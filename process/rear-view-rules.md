# Rear reconstruction review

Rear views must describe a garment seen from behind, including the volume of the invisible torso. A rear neckline alone does not make a front-shaped torso a convincing back.

When correcting an existing rear image, use that rear image as the edit reference. Preserve its garment construction, fabric, colors, framing, scale, lighting and background. Do not feed a front render into an anatomy-only correction.

Ask for a broad, relatively flat upper-back plane, natural shoulder-blade drape and a gentle taper toward the waist. Preserve realistic depth without paired chest domes, cup contours, underbust shadows or breast-shaped side projections. A back should not become a flat paper cutout.

Inspect each output directly. Check the silhouette and fabric shading as well as the neckline, straps and seams. Distinguish actual rear design panels and gathers from anatomy artifacts; leave already plausible images unchanged.

Keep inferred rear construction labelled as inferred. An anatomy correction does not verify historical details. Retain any existing rear-photo reference and its attribution.

Publish corrected images with fresh filenames and update the rear pairing hashes so browsers load the reviewed revision.
